THE SATURN EXPLORER
Cassini was a robotic spacecraft launched in 1997 as part of a joint NASA, ESA and Italian Space Agency mission. After a journey of almost seven years, Cassini entered orbit around Saturn in 2004. For more than a decade, the spacecraft studied Saturn, its rings, atmosphere and many moons in unprecedented detail. One of its most important discoveries came at Enceladus, where Cassini detected evidence of a global subsurface ocean and water-rich plumes erupting from the moon's south polar region. Cassini also carried the Huygens probe, which descended through the atmosphere of Titan and landed on its surface in 2005. In 2017, Cassini ended its mission by deliberately entering Saturn's atmosphere. This final phase became known as the Grand Finale.
CASSINI DISCOVERIES
TITAN
Cassini revealed Titan's complex surface, including lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane. The Huygens probe also landed there in 2005.
ENCELADUS
Cassini discovered powerful water-rich plumes erupting from Enceladus, revealing a hidden ocean beneath its icy crust.
SATURN'S RINGS
The spacecraft studied the rings in detail, revealing their structure, composition, waves and interactions with Saturn's moons.
DID YOU KNOW?
Cassini's final mission phase sent the spacecraft between Saturn and its innermost ring before it burned up in Saturn's atmosphere.
