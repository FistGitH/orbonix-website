BEYOND PLUTO
New Horizons was launched by NASA in January 2006 on a mission to explore Pluto and the distant Kuiper Belt. After a journey of more than nine years, the spacecraft flew past Pluto on July 14, 2015, becoming the first spacecraft to explore the dwarf planet up close. New Horizons revealed enormous mountains, vast icy plains, deep canyons and a surprisingly complex atmosphere. The spacecraft then continued deeper into the Kuiper Belt. On January 1, 2019, it performed the first close flyby of a Kuiper Belt object, Arrokoth. The mission continues to travel through the outer Solar System and study the environment far beyond Pluto.
NEW HORIZONS DISCOVERIES
PLUTO
New Horizons revealed Pluto's diverse surface, including Sputnik Planitia, mountains of water ice and possible evidence of geological activity.
ARROKOTH
The spacecraft flew past Arrokoth in 2019, providing humanity's first close look at a primordial Kuiper Belt object.
KUIPER BELT
New Horizons is continuing to explore the distant outer Solar System and study dust, plasma and the environment beyond Pluto.
DID YOU KNOW?
New Horizons crossed the distance from Earth to Pluto in just over nine years and reached Pluto at about 14 km/s.
