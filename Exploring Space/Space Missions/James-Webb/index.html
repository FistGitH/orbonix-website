THE NEXT GENERATION TELESCOPE
The James Webb Space Telescope, usually called JWST, was launched on December 25, 2021. Unlike the Hubble Space Telescope, Webb primarily observes the universe in infrared wavelengths. Its enormous 6.5-meter segmented primary mirror allows it to collect far more light than previous space telescopes. Webb operates near the second Sun–Earth Lagrange point, known as L2, approximately 1.5 million kilometres from Earth. From this location, the telescope can maintain a very cold environment and observe extremely faint infrared objects.
JAMES WEBB FEATURES
EARLY UNIVERSE
Webb can observe extremely distant galaxies whose light has travelled for more than 13 billion years.
EXOPLANETS
The telescope can analyze the atmospheres of some exoplanets and search for chemical signatures.
INFRARED VISION
Infrared observations allow Webb to see through some cosmic dust and study objects invisible in ordinary visible light.
DID YOU KNOW?
Webb's primary mirror is made of 18 gold-coated segments, working together as one enormous 6.5-meter telescope.
