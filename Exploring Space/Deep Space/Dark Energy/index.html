WHAT IS DARK ENERGY?

Dark energy is the name scientists give to whatever is causing the expansion of the Universe to accelerate. It is not known to be a conventional substance like atoms or ordinary matter. Instead, it may be a property of space itself or something associated with a new field of physics.

THE UNIVERSE IS EXPANDING

The Universe has been expanding since the early Universe. For a long time, astronomers expected gravity to gradually slow that expansion. Observations of distant Type Ia supernovae in the late 1990s revealed something surprising: cosmic expansion is accelerating.

DARK ENERGY IS NOT DARK MATTER

Despite their similar names, dark energy and dark matter are very different. Dark matter behaves as an unseen source of gravity that helps form galaxies and large-scale structure. Dark energy is associated with the accelerating expansion of the Universe.

THE COSMOLOGICAL CONSTANT

One leading explanation is Einstein's cosmological constant, usually written as Λ. In this model, empty space itself has a constant energy density. As the Universe expands, this component continues to affect the overall expansion.

WHAT WE KNOW ABOUT DARK ENERGY
ACCELERATED EXPANSION
Distant observations show that the expansion of the Universe is speeding up.
COSMIC SCALE
Dark energy becomes increasingly important as the Universe expands and matter becomes more spread out.
SUPERNOVAE
Type Ia supernovae helped reveal that cosmic expansion is accelerating.
COSMIC MICROWAVE BACKGROUND
Measurements of the early Universe help constrain models of dark energy.
GALAXY DISTRIBUTION
The large-scale distribution of galaxies provides another way to study cosmic expansion.
BIG MYSTERY
Scientists still do not know whether dark energy is a cosmological constant or something more complicated.
DID YOU KNOW?
The expansion of the Universe is not simply continuing — it is accelerating. And the physical cause of that acceleration remains one of the biggest unanswered questions in modern cosmology.
