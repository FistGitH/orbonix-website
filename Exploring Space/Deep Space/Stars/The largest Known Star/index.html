WHAT IS STEPHENSON 2-18?

Stephenson 2-18 is a red supergiant star associated with the Stephenson 2 stellar cluster in the constellation Scutum. It is frequently listed among the largest known stars by radius. Estimates of its physical size are highly uncertain because its distance, cluster membership and atmospheric properties are difficult to determine precisely.

HOW LARGE IS IT?

Some estimates place Stephenson 2-18 at roughly 2,000 times the radius of the Sun, with values around 2,150 solar radii often quoted. If such an estimate were correct, its photosphere would extend enormously beyond the orbit of Mars if the star replaced the Sun. However, the exact radius should not be treated as a precisely measured value.

WHY IS ITS SIZE UNCERTAIN?

Determining the radius of a distant red supergiant is difficult. Astronomers must estimate its distance, temperature, luminosity and atmospheric properties. Stephenson 2-18 is especially challenging because its relationship with the Stephenson 2 cluster is not completely certain. For this reason, astronomers should describe its extreme size as an estimate rather than an exact measurement.

WHAT WILL HAPPEN TO THE STAR?

A massive red supergiant is in a late stage of stellar evolution. Eventually, its core can collapse when it can no longer support itself against gravity. Depending on the star's final core mass, the collapse could produce a powerful core-collapse supernova and leave behind a neutron star or black hole.
