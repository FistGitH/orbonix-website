THE ROCKY FRONTIER
The asteroid belt is a vast region of the Solar System located between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. It contains millions of rocky and metallic bodies known as asteroids. Most are relatively small, but the region also contains large objects such as Ceres, Vesta, Pallas and Hygiea. Despite its name, the asteroid belt is not a densely packed field of rocks. The average distance between individual asteroids is enormous, making collisions between spacecraft and asteroids very rare. Jupiter's powerful gravity prevented the material in this region from combining into a planet during the formation of the Solar System. Instead, the asteroid belt remained as one of the best surviving records of the early Solar System.
ASTEROID BELT FEATURES
CERES
Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and is classified as a dwarf planet.
JUPITER'S GRAVITY
Jupiter's enormous gravity disrupted the formation of a planet in this region of the Solar System.
ANCIENT MATERIAL
Asteroids preserve material left over from the formation of the Solar System more than 4.5 billion years ago.
DID YOU KNOW?
The asteroid belt contains millions of objects, but spacecraft can travel through it without constantly dodging asteroids.
