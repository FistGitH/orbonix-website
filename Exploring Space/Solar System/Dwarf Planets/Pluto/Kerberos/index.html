KERBEROS

A small, irregular moon of Pluto, named after the three-headed guardian of the underworld in Greek mythology.
THE SMALL MOON
Kerberos is one of the five known moons of Pluto. It was discovered in 2011 using the Hubble Space Telescope and was originally designated S/2011 (134340) 1. The moon is very small and has an irregular shape. Images from the New Horizons mission showed that Kerberos appears to consist of two connected lobes, giving it a distinctive shape. Kerberos orbits Pluto beyond the orbit of Nix and before the orbit of Hydra. Because of its small size and distance, very little is known about its surface and internal structure.

KERBEROS FEATURES
TWO LOBES
Kerberos appears to have a distinctive two-lobed shape, possibly formed when two smaller bodies merged together.
NEW HORIZONS
The spacecraft photographed Kerberos during its historic flyby of the Pluto system in 2015.
MYTHOLOGY
Kerberos is named after Cerberus, the multi-headed guardian of the underworld in Greek mythology.
DID YOU KNOW?
Kerberos is so small that it is only about 20 kilometres across, yet it is one of Pluto's five known moons.
