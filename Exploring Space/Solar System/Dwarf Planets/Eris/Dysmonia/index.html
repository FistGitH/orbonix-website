THE MOON OF ERIS
Dysnomia is the only known natural satellite of the dwarf planet Eris. It was discovered in 2005, shortly after Eris itself was identified. The moon is much smaller than Eris and orbits the dwarf planet at a distance of roughly 37,000 kilometres. Its orbit takes approximately 15.8 Earth days. Dysnomia is extremely distant from the Sun, making detailed observations of its surface very difficult. Scientists use observations of Dysnomia's orbit to determine the mass of Eris. This makes the moon particularly important for studying the Eris system.
DYSNOMIA FEATURES

MEASURING ERIS

DID YOU KNOW?
Without Dysnomia's orbit, astronomers would have much more difficulty determining the mass of Eris.
