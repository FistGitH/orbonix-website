THE RED DWARF PLANET
Gonggong is a large dwarf planet located in the distant Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune. It was discovered in 2007 and was originally known by the provisional designation 2007 OR10. The object has a strongly reddish surface, which is thought to be caused by complex organic materials called tholins that have been altered by radiation over extremely long periods of time. Gonggong follows a highly elliptical orbit around the Sun and takes more than five centuries to complete one revolution. The dwarf planet has one known moon, Xiangliu, which was discovered in 2016. Gonggong is one of the largest known objects in the Kuiper Belt and is thought to contain significant amounts of water ice beneath or within its surface layers.
GONGGONG FEATURES
RED SURFACE
Gonggong has one of the reddest surfaces known among large objects in the outer Solar System.
LONG ORBIT
Gonggong needs roughly 554 Earth years to complete a single orbit around the Sun.
XIANG LIU
Gonggong has a known natural satellite called Xiangliu, discovered using the Hubble Space Telescope.
DID YOU KNOW?
One year on Gonggong lasts about 554 Earth years. That means a single Gonggong year is longer than all of recorded human history.
