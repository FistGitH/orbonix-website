THE JOURNEY TO THE MOON
The Apollo program was a series of human spaceflight missions conducted by NASA between 1961 and 1972. Its primary goal was to land humans on the Moon and return them safely to Earth. Apollo 11 achieved the historic first crewed Moon landing on July 20, 1969, when Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the lunar surface while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit. The Apollo missions returned hundreds of kilograms of lunar rocks and soil to Earth, allowing scientists to study the Moon in unprecedented detail. Apollo 13 demonstrated another side of space exploration. After an oxygen tank explosion damaged the spacecraft, the crew survived a dangerous journey back to Earth. The final lunar mission, Apollo 17, ended in December 1972 and remains the most recent time humans have walked on the Moon.
