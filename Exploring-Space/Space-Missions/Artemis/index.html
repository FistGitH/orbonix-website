RETURN TO THE MOON
Artemis is a human spaceflight program focused on exploring the Moon and building the capabilities needed for future deep-space missions. The program uses NASA's Space Launch System rocket and Orion spacecraft, along with commercial lunar landers and other international and commercial partners. Artemis I launched in November 2022 without astronauts aboard. The mission tested the SLS rocket and Orion spacecraft during a journey around the Moon and back to Earth. Future Artemis missions are intended to bring astronauts back to the lunar surface and establish a sustained human presence around and on the Moon. The experience gained from lunar missions is also intended to help prepare humanity for future crewed missions to Mars.
ARTEMIS MISSION FEATURES
SLS ROCKET
The Space Launch System is a powerful heavy-lift rocket designed to send Orion and crew toward the Moon and beyond.
ORION
Orion is the crew spacecraft designed to transport astronauts through deep space and return them safely to Earth.
LUNAR GATEWAY
Gateway is a planned space station in lunar orbit that will support exploration and future missions to the Moon.
DID YOU KNOW?
Artemis I traveled around the Moon and reached a distance of more than 430,000 km from Earth during its mission.
