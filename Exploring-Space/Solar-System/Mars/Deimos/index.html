MARS'S OUTER MOON
Deimos is the smaller and more distant of Mars's two natural satellites. Like Phobos, Deimos has an irregular shape and a heavily cratered surface. It is much smaller and smoother-looking than Phobos because its surface is covered by a layer of loose material called regolith. Deimos orbits Mars approximately every 30.3 hours and travels in the same general direction as Mars's rotation. From Mars, Deimos would appear as a small, slow-moving object in the night sky. The origin of Deimos is still not completely understood. One possibility is that it may be a captured asteroid.
SMALL MOON
Deimos is only about 12 kilometres across, making it one of the smallest known natural satellites of a planet.
DISTANT SATELLITE
Deimos orbits farther from Mars than Phobos and takes about 30 hours to complete one orbit.
IRREGULAR WORLD
Its irregular shape and heavily cratered surface give Deimos an appearance more similar to a small asteroid than a planet.
DID YOU KNOW?
Deimos is so small that its escape velocity is only about 5.6 m/s.
