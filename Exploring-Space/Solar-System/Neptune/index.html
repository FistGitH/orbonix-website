THE ICE GIANT

Neptune is the eighth planet from the Sun and the most distant major planet in our Solar System. Its striking blue color is caused mainly by methane in its atmosphere, which absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight. Neptune has one of the most active atmospheres in the Solar System, with winds that can reach more than 2,000 km/h. The planet has a faint ring system and 16 known moons. Triton is by far the largest and most famous of them.

MAJOR MOONS

TRITON
Neptune's largest moon, with an active icy surface and nitrogen geysers.
NEREID
A distant irregular moon with one of the most eccentric known orbits.
PROTEUS
A dark, irregular moon heavily covered with impact craters.
LARISSA
A small inner moon discovered in Voyager 2 images.
GALATEA
A small irregular moon that helps shape Neptune's faint ring system.
EXTREME WINDS
Neptune has some of the fastest planetary winds measured in the Solar System.
DEEP BLUE
Methane absorbs red light, helping give Neptune its deep blue appearance.
GREAT DARK SPOTS
Large dark storms have been observed moving through Neptune's atmosphere.
DID YOU KNOW?
One year on Neptune lasts about 165 Earth years. Since its discovery in 1846, Neptune has completed only one full orbit around the Sun.

WEATHER AT THE EDGE OF THE PLANETARY SYSTEM

Neptune receives far less sunlight than the inner planets, yet its atmosphere contains some of the fastest winds measured in the Solar System. Clouds and large storm systems move through an atmosphere composed mainly of hydrogen and helium with methane that contributes to its blue appearance.

Its largest moon, Triton, follows a retrograde orbit and is likely a captured object from the outer Solar System. The Neptune system therefore connects the study of giant planets with the icy populations beyond them.
