THE VOLCANIC MOON
Io is the innermost of Jupiter's four large Galilean moons. It is the most volcanically active world known in the Solar System. Hundreds of volcanic centers and enormous lava flows cover its surface. The extreme volcanic activity is caused by tidal heating. Jupiter's enormous gravity constantly stretches and compresses Io while the gravitational influence of Europa and Ganymede keeps its orbit slightly eccentric. This continuous flexing generates enormous amounts of heat inside Io. Some volcanic plumes can rise hundreds of kilometers above the surface.
EXTREME VOLCANISM
Io has hundreds of active volcanic centers and constantly changing lava flows.
TIDAL HEATING
Jupiter's gravity repeatedly deforms Io, generating heat deep inside the moon.
COLORFUL SURFACE
Sulfur and sulfur compounds create Io's distinctive yellow, orange, red and green surface colors.
