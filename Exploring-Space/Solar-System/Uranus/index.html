THE ICE GIANT

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and one of the two ice giants in our Solar System. Its blue-green color comes mainly from methane in the upper atmosphere, which absorbs red wavelengths of sunlight. Uranus is famous for its extreme axial tilt. Its rotation axis is tilted by about 98 degrees, making the planet appear to rotate almost on its side. The planet has a faint ring system and many known moons. Five of its largest moons are Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania and Oberon.

A PLANET ROTATING ON ITS SIDE

Uranus has an axial tilt of about 98 degrees, so it rotates almost on its side compared with most planets. This unusual orientation produces extreme seasonal illumination as Uranus travels around the Sun during its long orbit.

The planet also has a ring system, a magnetic field that is strongly tilted relative to its rotation axis, and a family of icy moons. Miranda is particularly notable for its varied terrain, including enormous fault systems and unusual geological structures.
