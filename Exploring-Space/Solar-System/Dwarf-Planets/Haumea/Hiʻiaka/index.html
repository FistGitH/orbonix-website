HAUMEA'S TWO KNOWN MOONS
THE LARGER MOON
Hiʻiaka is the largest known moon of Haumea and the outer of Haumea's two known natural satellites. It was discovered in 2005 by a team of astronomers led by Mike Brown and Chad Trujillo. Hiʻiaka orbits Haumea at a distance of almost 50,000 kilometres and takes about 49.5 Earth days to complete one revolution. The moon is thought to be largely icy, and its surface has spectral characteristics consistent with water ice. Its orbit and composition provide important clues about the unusual history of the Haumea system.
HIIAKA FEATURES
LARGER MOON
Hiʻiaka is considerably larger than Haumea's other known moon, Namaka.
WATER ICE
Observations indicate that Hiʻiaka's surface is rich in crystalline water ice.
HAUMEA SYSTEM
Studying Hiʻiaka helps scientists understand the unusual collisional history of the Haumea system.
DID YOU KNOW?
Hiʻiaka is roughly 310 kilometres across and is one of the largest known moons of a dwarf planet.
