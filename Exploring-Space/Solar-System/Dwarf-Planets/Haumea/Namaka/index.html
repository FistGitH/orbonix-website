HAUMEA'S TWO KNOWN MOONS
THE INNER MOON
Namaka is the smaller and inner moon of the dwarf planet Haumea. It was discovered in 2005 by a team of astronomers studying the distant Haumea system. Namaka orbits Haumea at an average distance of roughly 25,700 kilometres and completes one revolution in about 18 Earth days. Its orbit is unusual because it is strongly affected by Haumea's rapid rotation and by the gravitational influence of the larger moon Hiʻiaka. Like Hiʻiaka, Namaka is thought to contain a significant amount of water ice. The unusual orbits of Haumea's moons provide important evidence about the violent collision that may have created the Haumea system.
NAMAKA FEATURES
INNER MOON
Namaka is the inner of Haumea's two known natural satellites.
UNUSUAL ORBIT
Namaka follows an unusually tilted and eccentric orbit around Haumea.
WATER ICE
Observations suggest that Namaka's surface contains water ice.
DID YOU KNOW?
Namaka completes an orbit around Haumea in only about 18 days, despite being tens of thousands of kilometres from the dwarf planet.
