WHAT IS THE PHOTOSPHERE?
The photosphere is the layer that we normally see when we look at the Sun through properly protected solar observing equipment. It is not a solid surface. The Sun is made of plasma, so the photosphere is simply the depth from which most of the visible sunlight escaping the Sun originates. Its temperature is roughly 5,778 K (about 5,500 °C). The photosphere is also where many of the Sun's most recognizable features can be observed, including sunspots and granulation.
✦
GRANULATION
Millions of convection cells appear as bright and dark patterns across the solar surface. Each granule is typically around 1,000 km across.
●
SUNSPOTS
Dark regions caused by intense magnetic fields that inhibit convection and make the area cooler than its surroundings.

DID YOU KNOW?
