WHAT IS THE RADIATIVE ZONE?
The radiative zone extends outward from approximately 25% to 70% of the Sun's radius. Unlike the convective zone, material in this region does not transport energy primarily through large-scale convection. Instead, energy moves outward mainly through radiation. Photons are repeatedly absorbed and reemitted by particles in the extremely dense plasma. Their path is therefore far from a straight line. A photon can change direction countless times before its energy gradually moves toward the outer layers of the Sun.
ENERGY TRANSPORT
Energy generated by nuclear fusion in the core gradually diffuses outward. Photons interact with the surrounding plasma again and again, transferring energy toward the boundary with the convective zone.

DID YOU KNOW?
In the radiative zone, energy can take an enormous amount of time to travel outward because photons are constantly interacting with the plasma.
