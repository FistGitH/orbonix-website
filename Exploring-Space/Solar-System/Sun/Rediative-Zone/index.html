WHAT IS THE RADIATIVE ZONE?
The radiative zone extends outward from approximately 25% to 70% of the Sun's radius. Unlike the convective zone, material in this region does not transport energy primarily through large-scale convection. Instead, energy moves outward mainly through radiation. Photons are repeatedly absorbed, scattered and re-emitted by particles in the extremely dense plasma. Their paths are therefore far from straight lines. A photon can change direction countless times before its energy gradually moves toward the outer layers of the Sun. The transport of energy through this region is therefore best described as radiative diffusion.
