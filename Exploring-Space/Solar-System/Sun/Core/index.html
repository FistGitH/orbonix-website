INSIDE THE CORE
The solar core is the innermost part of the Sun and the only region where the conditions are extreme enough for sustained nuclear fusion. Temperatures reach roughly 15 million kelvin, while enormous gravitational pressure compresses the plasma to extremely high densities. Under these conditions, hydrogen nuclei can overcome their electrostatic repulsion and participate in the proton–proton chain reaction. The result is the production of helium, positrons, neutrinos and high-energy photons. The energy generated here ultimately powers the Sun's light and heat.
THE MAIN ENERGY SOURCE
4H → He + ENERGY
Four hydrogen nuclei are ultimately converted into one helium nucleus. A small amount of mass is converted into energy according to Einstein's mass–energy equivalence.
