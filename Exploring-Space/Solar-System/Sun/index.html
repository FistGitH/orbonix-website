WHAT IS THE SUN?
The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star located at the center of our Solar System. It contains about 99.8% of the total mass of the Solar System. Deep inside its core, hydrogen nuclei undergo nuclear fusion and form helium. This process releases enormous amounts of energy. That energy eventually reaches Earth as light and other electromagnetic radiation, providing the energy that supports life and drives Earth's climate. The Sun is also a highly active star. Its magnetic field constantly changes, producing sunspots, solar flares and coronal mass ejections.
