WHAT IS A GALAXY?

A galaxy is a huge gravitationally bound system containing stars, stellar remnants, gas, dust and dark matter. Galaxies range from relatively small dwarf galaxies to enormous systems containing hundreds of billions of stars. They can also contain supermassive black holes at their centers.

HOW DO GALAXIES FORM?

Galaxies began forming in the early Universe as matter gradually collected under gravity. Small structures merged together over billions of years, growing into larger galaxies. Dark matter played a major role by providing gravitational structures that helped ordinary matter gather and form stars and galaxies.

THE MILKY WAY

The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System. It is a barred spiral galaxy with a central region surrounded by a large disk of stars, gas and dust. The Solar System lies in one of its spiral-arm regions, roughly 26,000 light-years from the galactic center.

GALAXY COLLISIONS

Galaxies can interact and collide because of their mutual gravity. Despite the enormous number of stars inside galaxies, direct star-to-star collisions are extremely rare because the distances between stars are so large. The Milky Way and Andromeda are expected to interact in the distant future.

MAIN GALAXY TYPES
SPIRAL
Spiral galaxies have a rotating disk with spiral arms extending from a central bulge. The Milky Way and Andromeda are examples.
ELLIPTICAL
Elliptical galaxies have smooth, rounded shapes and generally contain older stars with relatively little cold gas and dust.
IRREGULAR
Irregular galaxies do not have a simple organized shape. Gravitational interactions can strongly influence their appearance.
LENTICULAR
Lenticular galaxies have a disk and central bulge like spiral galaxies, but they generally lack prominent spiral arms.
FAMOUS GALAXIES
MILKY WAY
Our home galaxy and a barred spiral galaxy containing the Solar System.
ANDROMEDA
A large spiral galaxy approximately 2.5 million light-years from Earth.
TRIANGULUM
A spiral galaxy in the Local Group and one of the nearest large galaxies to the Milky Way.
DID YOU KNOW?
When you look at a distant galaxy, you are looking into the past because its light can take millions or billions of years to reach Earth.
