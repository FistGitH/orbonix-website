WHAT IS A NEBULA?

A nebula is a huge region of gas and dust in space. Some nebulae are places where new stars are forming, while others are made from material expelled by dying stars. The gas can be illuminated by nearby stars, causing some nebulae to glow brilliantly across the night sky.

HOW DO STARS FORM?

Inside some dense molecular clouds, gravity slowly pulls gas and dust together. As material collapses, the center becomes hotter and denser. Eventually, a protostar forms and can develop into a new star. These stellar nurseries can contain thousands of young stars.

NEBULAE FROM DYING STARS

Not every nebula is a birthplace. When some stars reach the end of their lives, they eject material into space. Massive stars can explode as supernovae, creating expanding clouds of gas and dust. Other stars create planetary nebulae as they shed their outer layers.

WHY DO NEBULAE HAVE DIFFERENT COLORS?

Different gases emit light at different wavelengths. Hydrogen, oxygen and other elements can produce characteristic colors when they are energized by nearby stars. The colors seen in astronomical images can also be enhanced or assigned to different wavelengths to reveal structures that human eyes cannot see directly.

MAJOR TYPES OF NEBULAE
EMISSION
Hot, energized gas emits its own light. These nebulae can glow brightly around young, massive stars.
REFLECTION
Dust scatters light from nearby stars, creating a glowing cloud that shines mainly by reflected starlight.
DARK
Dense clouds of dust block light from objects behind them, appearing as dark silhouettes against brighter regions.
PLANETARY
Shells of gas expelled by dying low- to intermediate-mass stars.
SUPERNOVA REMNANT
Expanding debris and shock-heated gas left behind after a massive star explodes.
MOLECULAR CLOUD
Cold, dense regions rich in molecular gas and dust where new stars can form.
DID YOU KNOW?
The elements released by stars into nebulae can eventually become part of new stars, planets and other worlds. In that sense, nebulae are part of the cosmic cycle of matter.
