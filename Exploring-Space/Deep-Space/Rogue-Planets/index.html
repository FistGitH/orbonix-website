WHAT IS A ROGUE PLANET?

A rogue planet is a planetary-mass object that travels through space without being gravitationally bound to a star. Unlike Earth, Jupiter or Neptune, it does not follow a normal orbit around a host star. Some may have formed around stars and later been ejected from their planetary systems.

HOW DO THEY BECOME ROGUE?

Young planetary systems can be chaotic. When several massive planets interact gravitationally, one planet can receive enough energy to be thrown completely out of the system. After that, it can continue travelling through interstellar space for millions or billions of years.

CAN THEY FORM ALONE?

Scientists are investigating whether some planetary-mass objects could form directly from collapsing clouds of gas, without ever orbiting a star. The distinction between a very small brown dwarf and a rogue planet can therefore be complicated.

COULD THEY HAVE MOONS?

A rogue planet could potentially retain moons after leaving its original system. However, detecting such moons would be extremely difficult because both objects would be drifting through the darkness of interstellar space.

THE MYSTERY OF ROGUE PLANETS
NO SUNRISE
A rogue planet would not experience a normal sunrise or sunset because there is no nearby star illuminating its surface.
EXTREME COLD
Without stellar heating, the surface could become incredibly cold, although internal heat may remain for a long time.
HARD TO FIND
They emit very little visible light, making direct observation extremely difficult.
MICROLENSING
Astronomers can sometimes detect planetary-mass objects when their gravity briefly magnifies the light of a distant star.
ANCIENT WORLDS
Some rogue planets could have travelled through the Milky Way for billions of years.
UNKNOWN POPULATION
Astronomers are still determining how many rogue planets exist in our galaxy.
DID YOU KNOW?
A rogue planet could be travelling through the Milky Way with no star nearby to light its surface — a planetary world wandering alone through interstellar darkness.
