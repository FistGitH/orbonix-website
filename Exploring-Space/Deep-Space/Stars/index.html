ORBONIX · DEEP SPACE

STARS

From ordinary stars like our Sun to pulsars, magnetars, supernovae and enormous stellar giants, stars are among the most powerful objects in the Universe.
OUR STAR · THE SUN

Stars are massive balls of plasma held together by gravity. Deep inside their cores, nuclear fusion releases enormous amounts of energy and creates many of the elements found throughout the Universe.

EXPLORE THE STARS
01 · NEUTRON STAR

PULSAR

A pulsar is a rapidly rotating neutron star that emits beams of electromagnetic radiation. As the beam sweeps across Earth, astronomers can detect regular pulses.

RAPID ROTATION
02 · STELLAR EXPLOSION

SUPERNOVAE

A supernova is an enormous stellar explosion. Some occur when massive stars collapse, while others involve the thermonuclear destruction of white dwarfs.

COSMIC EXPLOSION
03 · STELLAR GIANTS

GIANT STARS

Giant and supergiant stars can become hundreds or even more than a thousand times larger than the Sun during later stages of stellar evolution.

MASSIVE STARS
04 · BRIGHTEST FROM EARTH

THE BRIGHTEST STAR

Sirius is the brightest star in Earth's night sky. It is actually a binary system located about 8.6 light-years away.

SIRIUS
05 · EXTREME STAR

THE LARGEST KNOWN STAR

Some red supergiants have enormous estimated radii. Their exact sizes are difficult to determine because their outer atmospheres are diffuse and variable.

RED SUPERGIANTS
06 · EXOTIC OBJECTS

STRANGE STARS

Strange stars are hypothetical compact objects that could contain strange quark matter. Their existence has not yet been confirmed.

HYPOTHETICAL
07 · EXTREME MAGNETISM

MAGNETARS

Magnetars are neutron stars with extraordinarily powerful magnetic fields. They can produce intense bursts of X-rays and gamma rays.

ULTRA-STRONG FIELD
08 · STELLAR REMNANT

WHITE DWARFS

A white dwarf is the dense remnant left behind by a low- or medium-mass star after it has exhausted its nuclear fuel.

STELLAR REMNANT
09 · SUBSTELLAR OBJECT

BROWN DWARFS

Brown dwarfs are objects too small to sustain ordinary hydrogen fusion like true stars, occupying a category between giant planets and stars.

FAILED STAR
DID YOU KNOW?
The Sun is only one ordinary star among hundreds of billions in the Milky Way — and the Universe contains vastly more galaxies beyond our own.
