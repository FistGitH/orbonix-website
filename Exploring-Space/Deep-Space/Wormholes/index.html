WHAT IS A WORMHOLE?

A wormhole is a hypothetical structure in spacetime that could connect two separate regions of the Universe. Instead of travelling through the ordinary space between two locations, a wormhole would represent a shortcut through the geometry of spacetime. Wormholes arise as possible mathematical solutions to Einstein's equations of general relativity.

EINSTEIN–ROSEN BRIDGE

In 1935, Albert Einstein and Nathan Rosen described a mathematical structure now known as an Einstein–Rosen bridge. It is often associated with the idea of a wormhole connecting different regions of spacetime. However, the original Einstein–Rosen bridge is not considered traversable.

COULD HUMANS TRAVEL THROUGH ONE?

No traversable wormhole has ever been observed. Some theoretical models suggest that a wormhole would collapse too quickly to allow anything to pass through it. Keeping a wormhole open may require exotic forms of matter or energy with properties that have not been shown to exist on the required scale.

WORMHOLES AND BLACK HOLES

Wormholes are sometimes confused with black holes because both appear in solutions of general relativity. A black hole is supported by strong observational evidence. A traversable wormhole, however, remains hypothetical and has never been detected.

KEY IDEAS
SPACETIME SHORTCUT
A wormhole could theoretically connect two distant points using a shorter path through spacetime.
TWO MOUTHS
A hypothetical traversable wormhole would have two entrances connected by a throat.
EXOTIC MATTER
Some models require negative energy density or other unusual conditions to keep a wormhole open.
TIME TRAVEL
Certain theoretical wormhole scenarios can produce unusual effects involving time, but this remains speculative.
NO OBSERVATIONS
Astronomers have not confirmed the existence of any wormhole.
GENERAL RELATIVITY
Wormholes emerge from mathematical solutions within Einstein's theory of gravity.
DID YOU KNOW?
A wormhole is not known to be a real cosmic tunnel — it is a fascinating possibility permitted by some solutions of Einstein's equations.
